# Home Cooking Essentials

## Kitchen Knife Skills

A sharp knife is safer than a dull one. A dull blade requires more force, increasing the chance of slipping. Hone your knives with a honing steel before each use; sharpen with a whetstone every 2–3 months depending on frequency.

**The three essential cuts:**

1. **Julienne**: cut vegetables into thin matchsticks (~3mm × 3mm × 5cm). Start by squaring the vegetable, then slice into planks, then stack and cut into sticks.

2. **Brunoise**: 3mm × 3mm × 3mm dice. Begin with julienne, rotate 90°, and cut crosswise.

3. **Chiffonade**: roll herbs or leafy greens into a tight cylinder and slice thinly. Used for basil, mint, and sage.

The **claw grip** protects your fingers: curl fingertips under and rest the flat side of the blade against the second knuckle.

## Maillard Reaction vs. Caramelization

Two distinct browning reactions are commonly confused:

**Maillard reaction**: occurs between amino acids and reducing sugars above 140°C (285°F). Produces hundreds of complex flavor compounds. Requires relatively dry surface — pat meat dry before searing. Salt draws out moisture; salt well in advance or immediately before cooking.

**Caramelization**: pure sugar decomposition, starting around 160°C (320°F) for sucrose. Does not require protein. Produces caramel flavor in onions, carrots, and desserts.

Both reactions require high temperatures and low moisture. Overcrowding a pan lowers the surface temperature and causes steaming instead of browning.

## Stock Fundamentals

Stock is the foundation of classical sauces. The ratio of cold water to bones is roughly **1:1 by weight**.

**Brown stock** (fond brun): roast bones at 220°C until golden before simmering. Adds depth from Maillard products. Used for demi-glace.

**White stock** (fond blanc): blanch bones in cold water, discard water, then simmer. Produces a clear, neutral-flavored liquid.

**Remouillage**: a second extraction from the same bones using the first stock as liquid — lighter in flavor, used to replace water in the initial stock.

Key rule: **never boil a stock** after it begins simmering. Boiling emulsifies fat and proteins into the liquid, producing a cloudy, greasy result. Keep it at a gentle simmer (small bubbles rising occasionally at the sides).

## Bread Baking: Gluten and Fermentation

Bread requires two processes:
1. **Gluten development**: kneading aligns glutenin and gliadin proteins into elastic networks that trap CO₂
2. **Fermentation**: yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) metabolizes sugars, producing CO₂ and ethanol

**Hydration** (water as a percentage of flour weight) determines crumb structure:
- 60–65%: sandwich loaves, tight crumb
- 70–75%: ciabatta, open irregular crumb
- 80%+: no-knead breads; requires long fermentation to develop gluten

**Autolyse**: resting hydrated flour before adding yeast or salt allows flour to fully hydrate and begin gluten development without mechanical work, reducing total kneading time.

## Emulsification

An emulsion is a stable mixture of two immiscible liquids (usually oil and water). The emulsifier reduces surface tension between the phases.

**Mayonnaise**: egg yolk lecithin emulsifies oil into acidulated water (lemon juice or vinegar). Add oil very slowly at first — large oil drops collapse the emulsion. Room-temperature ingredients emulsify more easily.

**Vinaigrette**: a temporary emulsion (breaks on standing). Dijon mustard contains mucilage that acts as an emulsifier and thickener. Ratio: 3 parts oil to 1 part acid.

**Butter sauce (beurre blanc)**: cold butter whisked into a wine/shallot reduction. The emulsion is fragile — maintain temperature between 60–80°C. Below 60°C, the butter solidifies and breaks; above 80°C, the fat separates.

## Temperature Control

### Meat Doneness

| Internal Temperature | Doneness |
|---------------------|----------|
| 52°C (125°F) | Rare |
| 57°C (135°F) | Medium rare |
| 63°C (145°F) | Medium |
| 71°C (160°F) | Well done |

Carry-over cooking raises internal temperature 3–8°C after removing from heat. Rest meat 5–10 minutes under loose foil before slicing.

### Food Safety

The **danger zone** for bacterial growth is 4–60°C (40–140°F). Refrigerate perishables within 2 hours; within 1 hour above 32°C ambient temperature.

Core temperature for food safety: poultry must reach 74°C (165°F); ground beef 71°C (160°F); whole pork cuts 63°C (145°F) with 3-minute rest.

## Seasoning Principles

Salt is the most impactful seasoning variable. Season in layers — adding salt early draws moisture from vegetables and proteins, concentrating flavor. Finishing salt (flaky sea salt) adds textural contrast.

Acid (citrus, vinegar) brightens dishes and counteracts richness. If a dish tastes flat, try acid before adding more salt. Fat carries flavor compounds and provides mouthfeel; adding a small amount of butter or olive oil at the end ("mounting") rounds out sauces.
